Module 6 · Lesson 13

Total Quality Management

TQM principles, Deming's 14 Points, Cost of Poor Quality

Goal

Describe the core principles of TQM, apply Deming's 14 Points to a modern data context, and compute the Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ).

Concept

TQM is a management philosophy where the entire organisation — from CEO to frontline — is committed to continuously improving quality. It shifts focus from inspection at the end of a line to building quality into every process.

Core TQM Principles

Customer Focus

All quality decisions start with the customer — internal and external.

Total Employee Involvement

Everyone is responsible for quality, not just the QC department.

Process-Centred

Manage and improve the process — good processes produce good outcomes.

Continuous Improvement (Kaizen)

Never accept the current state as the best possible state.

Fact-Based Decisions

Use data and statistical tools — not intuition alone.

Integrated System

All departments work toward common quality goals.

Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ)

CategoryData Engineering Example
PreventionData quality training, automated schema validation, audits
AppraisalDaily data quality checks, profiling tool licence
Internal FailureReprocessing failed jobs, correcting bad training data
External FailureRevenue loss from bad recommendations, SLA penalties

1:10:100 Rule: Every £1 spent on prevention saves ~£10 on internal failures and ~£100 on external failures. Prevention is always the cheapest category.

Example — Stale Product Data

A recommendation engine uses 3-month-old product data. Result:

  • External Failure: 12% drop in CTR → ~£80,000 revenue loss.
  • Internal Failure: 40 hours of engineering time to clean and reload data.
  • Prevention cost missing: Automated data freshness monitoring would have cost £500.

£500 in prevention would have saved £80,000+ in losses.
