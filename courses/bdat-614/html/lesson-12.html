Module 5 · Lesson 12

Lean Six Sigma

8 Lean wastes, Value Stream Mapping, Lean tools

Goal

Describe how Lean combines with Six Sigma, identify the 8 Lean wastes, and apply Value Stream Mapping to identify non-value-adding steps.

Concept

Six Sigma alone

Reduces variation — but may leave slow, wasteful processes intact

Lean alone

Speeds up processes — but may still produce defective output

Lean Six Sigma

Both: fast AND defect-free

The 8 Lean Wastes (TIM WOODS)

LetterWasteData Engineering Example
TTransportationMoving data between unnecessary systems
IInventoryRecords waiting in landing zone queues
MMotionAnalysts switching between 6 tools to do one task
WWaitingPipeline jobs waiting for upstream data for 4 hours
OOverproductionGenerating daily reports nobody reads
OOverprocessingRunning 12 validation steps when 4 suffice
DDefectsInvalid records requiring rework
SUnused TalentAnalysts doing manual work that could be automated

Value Stream Mapping (VSM)

Diagrams every step from supplier to customer and labels each as:

VA

Value-Adding — customer would pay for this

NVA

Non-Value-Adding — waste, eliminate it

NNVA

Required but not value-adding — minimise

Process Cycle Efficiency = VA time / Total time × 100%

# Typical: 5–15%. Lean target: > 25%

Example — Data Pipeline VSM

StepTimeType
Ingest from source5 minVA
Wait in queue45 minNVA — Waiting
Validate schema3 minVA
Manual approval30 minNVA — Overprocessing
Load to warehouse4 minVA

Total = 87 min. VA = 12 min. PCE = 14% — 86% is waste.

Task — Python Exercise

Given 10 process steps with times:

  1. Classify each step as VA, NVA, or NNVA.
  2. Compute Process Cycle Efficiency.
  3. Map each NVA step to a Lean waste category (TIM WOODS).
  4. Print a lean improvement recommendation.
