Module 4 · Lesson 9

DMAIC Framework

Root cause analysis — Fishbone diagram and Pareto analysis

Goal

Describe the five DMAIC phases, match quality tools to each phase, and use Pareto analysis to identify the dominant root cause of a quality problem.

Concept

DMAIC is the core problem-solving framework of Six Sigma. It is structured and data-driven:

PhaseQuestionKey Tools
DefineWhat is the problem?Project Charter, SIPOC, Voice of Customer
MeasureHow is it performing now?Control Charts, Gauge R&R, Baseline DPMO
AnalyzeWhat are the root causes?Fishbone, Pareto, Hypothesis Testing
ImproveHow can we fix it?DOE, Kaizen, Pilot
ControlHow do we sustain gains?Control Charts, Control Plans, Dashboards

Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagram

Visual tool for the Analyze phase. Maps all possible causes of a problem (the "effect") by the 6M categories. The problem is the "fish head"; each M is a bone. Use it to brainstorm hypotheses — then confirm with data.

[Man] [Machine] → → → → → → → [EFFECT: High Error Rate]
[Material] [Method] ↗

Pareto Chart — The 80/20 Rule

Ranks causes by frequency with a cumulative % line. Approximately 80% of problems come from 20% of causes. Fix the top 2–3 causes and you resolve most of the problem.

Example — Customer Complaints

Defect TypeCountCumulative %
Wrong delivery address20040%
Damaged packaging15070% ← Fix these two first
Missing item7585%
Wrong product5095%
Other25100%

Fix "Wrong delivery address" and "Damaged packaging" first — together they are 70% of all complaints with minimum effort.
